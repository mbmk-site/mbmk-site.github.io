How Political Instability in Middle-East and Ukraine affects household in India

Introduction

In the year 2024, there were 513,000 asylum seeker applications in the European Union (EU) from the Middle East and Ukraine. This number is considered “stable” by the European Union Agency for Asylum (EUAA) compared to the previous year, which saw the highest number of applications since the refugee crisis of 2015-16. According to the agency, the majority of applications came from the Middle East. While this data may seem hopeful, political instability in the Middle East and Ukraine continues to grow. The Israel-Hamas conflict has resulted in 49,000 casualties, including 138 innocent children, while the Russia-Ukraine war has claimed a staggering 1 million lives, including both soldiers and civilians.

The loss is not limited to human lives; it also impacts the global economy. Key exports from these regions have been severely disrupted due to the conflicts, affecting global production and consumption. While many may think these are issues faced only by governments and corporations, the reality is that every household on Earth is affected by these conflicts, especially in India, a major importer of petroleum and other products from these war-torn regions. This article will explore two products whose prices have surged due to these conflicts.

The Ever Increasing Price of Petroleum

The increase in petrol and diesel prices in India has been evident in recent years, affecting every individual in the country. The primary cause of this price hike is the ongoing conflicts in the Middle East, particularly the Gaza War between Israel, Hamas, and their allies, as well as the Houthi rebellion in Yemen. To understand this, let’s examine how oil is produced and transported to its destination.

Crude oil is produced and refined in nations like Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and Jordan by corporations such as Aramco. This oil is then transported through the Suez Canal, one of the most fragile yet crucial trade routes in the world. The Suez Canal is bordered by Egypt, a politically unstable nation led by a dictatorship to the west, and the Israel-Palestine conflict zone to the east. Due to this precarious geographic location, oil-exporting nations often reduce the flow of oil to avoid the risk of damage from attacks. This reduction in supply increases demand in importing nations like India, leading to higher oil prices.

Thus, the ongoing conflicts in the Middle East are the primary cause of the fluctuations in oil prices in India.

The Hike In the Price of One Everyday Commodity due to the Raging Conflicts

The conflicts are not only affecting the petroleum market in India but also the prices of everyday commodities, such as edible oil. Oils like soybean oil, mustard oil, and olive oil are not entirely produced in India but are imported from South America and Southern and Eastern Europe. For example, if you check the label on an olive oil bottle, it will likely state that it was produced in Spain or Italy and then imported to India. The reason for the price hike in these oils is the same as for petroleum: geopolitical tensions.

The largest exporters of mustard oil are Eastern European nations like Ukraine. Due to the ongoing conflict between Ukraine and Russia, many mustard oil production plants in Ukraine have been nearly or completely destroyed by Russian airstrikes. This destruction, occurring thousands of kilometers away, still impacts India. Additionally, the wars in the Middle East affect the transportation of edible oils, as these commodities are transported through the Suez Canal. Just as oil-exporting nations reduce the flow of petroleum due to high risk, the exporting nations of edible oils also cut back on exports, further increasing demand and prices in India. Other edible oils, such as soybean and olive oil, also experience similar price increases as they follow the same supply channels.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the fluctuations in the prices of these commodities affect every household and the overall economy of India. The increased cost of these commodities raises import costs, potentially leading to higher inflation. There are two primary ways for India to address this issue: becoming self-sufficient or advocating for an end to these costly conflicts. The first option is not entirely feasible due to environmental limitations, as India’s climate is not suitable for producing certain edible oils, and the country lacks significant crude oil reserves. Therefore, the more realistic approach is to advocate for ending these conflicts or negotiating special agreements to ensure the safe transport of these commodities. However, given the complexity of India’s foreign relations, achieving this will be challenging. Nonetheless, there is hope for a better future, especially with the recent truce between Hamas and Israel, which may bring some positive changes.
